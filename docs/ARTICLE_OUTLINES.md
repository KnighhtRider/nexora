# Resource Article Outlines

These are editorial outlines for the three proposed launch articles in `PAGE_BLUEPRINTS.md`. They are not publication-ready articles.

## 1. Why enterprise knowledge stays fragmented, and what retrieval changes

- **Working title:** Why Enterprise Knowledge Stays Fragmented—and What Retrieval Changes
- **Description:** A practical explanation of how teams can think about finding relevant context across approved organizational sources, and where retrieval could fit.
- **Author:** Pending human assignment; suggested role: subject-matter author with enterprise knowledge or information retrieval experience.
- **Date:** Pending publication planning.
- **Tags:** knowledge, retrieval, governance
- **Draft:** `true`
- **Claims to check:** C-002, C-007, C-008

### Outline

1. **Start with a work question.** Show a hypothetical employee trying to find the current procedure across documents, without claiming this is universal.
2. **Describe the sources.** Explain how a team's approved documents and systems may hold different pieces of context; avoid naming supported integrations.
3. **Explain retrieval in plain language.** Describe selecting relevant passages for a question, and distinguish that proposed capability from a guarantee of correctness.
4. **Make governance part of the flow.** Cover source permissions, least-privilege access and why answers should retain source references.
5. **Keep a person in control.** Show a reader checking a cited source before acting on an answer.
6. **Connect to the NEXORA concept.** Label Intelligence as a proposed module and describe retrieval as a potential use case, not an available feature.
7. **Close with evaluation questions.** Ask which sources are approved, how access is governed, and how people can verify answers.

**Editorial guardrails:** No market statistics, customer examples, unsupported accuracy claims, named integrations or claims that retrieval eliminates fragmentation.

## 2. Human-in-the-loop design for automated approvals

- **Working title:** Human-in-the-Loop Design for Automated Approvals
- **Description:** A design guide to placing clear human review points in proposed automated workflows.
- **Author:** Pending human assignment; suggested role: product or operations author familiar with approval workflows.
- **Date:** Pending publication planning.
- **Tags:** human-oversight, workflow, governance
- **Draft:** `true`
- **Claims to check:** C-003, C-004, C-006, C-007

### Outline

1. **Define the decision boundary.** Separate steps a proposed workflow may prepare from decisions that need human review.
2. **Choose review triggers.** Use a hypothetical invoice policy check to illustrate an exception that should wait for a person.
3. **Give reviewers useful context.** Show the source information, relevant rule and proposed next step together.
4. **Make the decision visible.** Describe possible outcomes such as approve, reject, request information or escalate, without promising a particular implementation.
5. **Keep a reviewable record.** Explain why recording the action and its context supports later review; do not claim compliance or guaranteed auditability.
6. **Connect to the NEXORA concept.** Map the example to proposed Flow coordination and human approval steps.
7. **Close with design questions.** Ask who reviews, what requires escalation, and how an unclear case is handled.

**Editorial guardrails:** Do not say human review removes risk, guarantees correctness or ensures compliance. Do not invent approval rates or time savings.

## 3. From documents to decisions: how invoice-style workflows are structured

- **Working title:** From Documents to Decisions: How Invoice-Style Workflows Are Structured
- **Description:** A step-by-step conceptual walkthrough of document intake, checks, exceptions and human review in an invoice-style workflow.
- **Author:** Pending human assignment; suggested role: operations author familiar with document workflows.
- **Date:** Pending publication planning.
- **Tags:** document-processing, invoices, workflow
- **Draft:** `true`
- **Claims to check:** C-003, C-004, C-006, C-008

### Outline

1. **Introduce the sample.** State that the walkthrough uses fictional sample data and is explanatory, not a real invoice or payment process.
2. **Describe intake.** Explain how a hypothetical document could enter a workflow, with no named vendor or integration claim.
3. **Extract and check.** Outline possible field extraction and policy checks as proposed steps; flag uncertain values for review.
4. **Route exceptions.** Show how a hypothetical mismatch could pause for human attention rather than silently continue.
5. **Record the steps.** Describe a reviewable sequence of inputs, checks and decisions as a design goal.
6. **Connect to the NEXORA concept.** Label Agents and Flow as proposed modules and point to the deterministic interactive simulation using sample data.
7. **Close with implementation questions.** Ask which fields, policies, reviewers and escalation paths a team would define.

**Editorial guardrails:** No invented statistics, real payment claims, real ERP activity, customer outcomes or assertions that the simulation uses an AI model.
